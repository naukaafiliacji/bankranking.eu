How to compare everyday bank accounts

A current account is the centre of day-to-day banking: salary or other incoming payments, cards, transfers, cash withdrawals and recurring bills. The cheapest account is not always the best one, because a low headline fee can be offset by card, ATM, foreign-exchange or transfer charges.

Monthly cost

Check both the account fee and the recurring fee for the debit card. If a fee is waived, note exactly what salary, balance or card-use condition must be met.

Payments and cash

Compare instant and standard transfers, local ATM access, cash deposits, mobile payments and any limits on free withdrawals.

Travel and FX

Look at card exchange rates, foreign-currency mark-ups, non-euro ATM charges and whether the bank offers travel packages or multi-currency features.

Service and eligibility

Check identity and residence requirements, branch access, app support, joint-account options and whether the product is genuinely available to a new customer.

Questions to answer before choosing

  • What is the realistic annual maintenance cost for the way I will use the account?
  • Which transactions are free and which are charged separately?
  • Does a reward require salary payments, direct debits or minimum card spend?
  • Can I keep the account on the same terms after a promotional period ends?

What can go wrong

A welcome bonus can be valuable, but it should not hide a costly long-term tariff. Compare the ongoing account after the reward period and treat optional credit, overdraft and insurance products separately.

Protection and product structure. Money held as an eligible deposit with an EU credit institution is generally covered by the relevant national deposit-guarantee scheme up to the harmonised EU limit. Always confirm which legal entity holds the deposit and which scheme applies.
