How to compare fixed-term deposits

A fixed-term deposit normally pays a stated rate in exchange for leaving money untouched for a defined period. The best rate is only useful if the maturity date matches when you may need the cash.

Term and rate

Compare annualised rates together with the exact term. A higher rate on a long lock-up is not directly equivalent to a shorter deposit.

Early withdrawal

Check whether early closure is allowed, whether interest is forfeited and whether any additional penalty applies.

Minimum and maximum

Products can impose a minimum deposit, a cap on the promotional rate or different rates for larger balances.

Maturity instructions

Find out whether the deposit repays automatically, rolls into a new term or moves to a low-interest account at maturity.

Questions to answer before choosing

  • Can I access the money before maturity?
  • Is the rate fixed for the entire term?
  • What happens automatically on the maturity date?
  • Does the deposit require a linked current account?

What can go wrong

Avoid locking emergency cash into a term deposit simply to gain a slightly higher rate. Liquidity has value, especially when early withdrawal is impossible or wipes out the interest.

Protection and product structure. Eligible fixed-term bank deposits are generally within the EU deposit-guarantee framework up to €100,000 per depositor per bank. Check the depositor information sheet and the legal bank entity.
