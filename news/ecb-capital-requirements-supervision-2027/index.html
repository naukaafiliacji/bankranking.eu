ECB sets its banking supervision priorities for 2027–29

Capital guidance remains broadly steady, while supervisors focus on cyber risk and AI.

Event: 19 November 2026 · Written: 23 November 2026 · BankRanking Europe

The announcement

On 19 November 2026, ECB Banking Supervision published its annual Supervisory Review and Evaluation Process (SREP) results and supervisory priorities for 2027–2029. The exercise covered 108 banks directly supervised by the ECB.

The combined Common Equity Tier 1 requirement and guidance for 2027 was broadly unchanged at 11.3% of risk-weighted assets, versus 11.2% for 2026. The ECB reported that the banks remained generally well capitalised and liquid, but identified operational and technology risks as areas requiring closer supervision.

What it means for account customers

The priorities focus on resilience to geopolitical and macro-financial shocks, cyber threats, reliance on third-party providers and the risks associated with banks’ use of AI. This does not mean an immediate change to a customer’s current account fee or the interest on a savings deposit.

For customers, the practical questions remain whether their bank provides reliable digital services, clear information about complaints and product fees, and robust security. Supervisory capital requirements are a bank-level safeguard, not a personal savings rate or a guarantee that every service disruption can be prevented.
