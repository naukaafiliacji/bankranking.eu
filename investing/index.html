How to compare investing services

Investment services differ in what you can buy, how much advice you receive and how fees are charged. A low platform fee can still be expensive if trading, fund, currency or custody charges are high for your portfolio.

Service model

Separate self-directed brokerage, managed portfolios, fund platforms and advice. They solve different problems and should not be ranked on price alone.

Total cost

Combine platform or custody fees with dealing commissions, fund charges, FX costs, transfer-out fees and any advisory fee.

Investment range

Check the markets, funds, ETFs, bonds or other instruments available and whether the provider restricts recurring investing or fractional holdings.

Tax and reporting

Consider local tax wrappers, statements and reporting support. The same investment platform can be much easier to use in one country than another.

Questions to answer before choosing

  • Do I want to choose investments myself or delegate portfolio decisions?
  • What would the total annual cost be for my expected portfolio size and trading activity?
  • Can I transfer assets out without selling them?
  • Which investor-protection regime applies to this provider?

What can go wrong

Investment returns are uncertain and can be negative. Compare costs and service, but do not treat a ranking as a prediction of future performance.

Protection and product structure. Investment assets are not the same as bank deposits. Investor-compensation and asset-segregation rules vary by provider and jurisdiction, and they do not protect against normal market losses.
