How to compare self-directed investing

Self-directed platforms give the customer control over security selection and trading. They can be inexpensive, but responsibility for diversification, timing and risk management remains with the investor.

Dealing charges

Compare commissions, minimum fees, exchange charges and the cost of recurring investment plans.

FX and market access

Check currency-conversion spreads and which exchanges, ETFs, shares, bonds or derivatives can be traded.

Tools and data

Consider order types, portfolio reporting, research, alerts and whether the interface supports the strategy you intend to use.

Custody and transfers

Review custody fees, securities lending, transfer-out charges and how client assets are segregated.

Questions to answer before choosing

  • How often will I trade and in which currencies?
  • Does the platform offer the exact securities and exchanges I need?
  • What costs appear beyond the advertised commission?
  • Can I transfer the portfolio to another broker later?

What can go wrong

Easy trading can encourage excessive activity. A low per-trade fee is not a reason to trade more often, and complex products can create losses well beyond what a beginner expects.

Protection and product structure. Investor-protection arrangements do not cover normal price movements. Check how the broker holds client assets and cash, and which regulator and compensation framework apply.
